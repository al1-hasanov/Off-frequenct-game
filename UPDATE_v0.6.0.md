# Off Frequency v0.6.0: The Futures (Act 3)

Act 3 is playable, start to finish: about 40 minutes after the mine tunnel, with two new choices. Every year the story visits now looks like that year: its buildings, its rooms, its cars, its people at the right age.

## What you'll see in each year

### Tonight, October 14, 1983

- **Outside, at the dish (new).** The summit in the snow: the 26-metre dish swung round to the horizon on its own (it's parked straight up when you park it), the braced yoke and the ladder up to the feed cabin, the concrete control building with its ribbon windows, the old stone wing, Mara's late-'70s sedan snowed in to the door handles, a sodium lamp over the lot, Halvard's lights across the valley, the Air Force padlock on the dish's main disconnect.
- **The control room** has Act 3's machinery: Dad's patch bay on the transmitter (the cables appear once patched), a chart recorder whose pen climbs while the dish listens and goes flat when it's parked, the dish drive panel (PARK / RELEASE), a taller chalkboard with *2.14 RUN · OCT 25* and the years Mara writes (and the ones that write themselves). Through the window you can see the dish turn, park, and swing back; at the end of the act, Halvard's lights go out a street at a time and the white comes over the ridge.
- A fire-axe case in the east corridor; the basement wall behind the boiler breaks open.

### 2031: the Halvard Signal Visitor Center

- **Outside:** an October afternoon, maples turning, leaves blowing. The old building has a glass-and-timber entrance, solar panels on the roof and banners (*LISTENING SINCE 1971*). An electric crossover charges at a post in a green bay, a driverless shuttle waits at the stop, wind turbines turn on the far ridge, the dish is painted white and parked at the zenith behind a fence with an interpretive sign. Visitors in puffer jackets and hoodies whisper, one staring at a phone with no signal.
- **Inside:** charcoal walls, LED track lights, polished concrete, a digital clock over the exit. The recordings wall (cassettes, answering machines, reel boxes), an oak card index, the transcript of the 2:14 transmission, Sollen's 1971 desk behind a rope on brass stanchions, the 2.14 study with a sepia photo of Captain Anders Lind and the clipping about the road, a gift-shop rack of silent radios under a screen that says NO SIGNAL, the brass plaque by the old control-room door.

### 2009: the lamp line

- The concrete building is a roofless shell full of snow; through the fallen front, the lobby wall says THEY CAME OUT OF THE DARK / COUNT YOURSELF. The stone wing is lit in every window, one dark room bricked up, a string of bulbs along the eaves, hurricane lamps on posts with a rope between them. Two cars from the 2000s abandoned in the lot (rust, cracked glass, flat tyres, drifts), the dish rusted fast with snow in the bowl, Tomas's corrugated hut with its lit window and woodpile, and a wall of white on the far ridge that comes nearer every time you do something.
- **Under the stone wing:** the same basement as tonight, 26 years on. The stairs bricked up, the coal chute, fallen shelves, Mara's crate rotted to slats (TOMAS nearly gone), the boiler rusted cold, the plaster crumbled to show the tin. You can only see what's inside Tomas's lamplight.

### 1991: half erased

- **Main Street, eight years on**, torn like a photograph: white past the end of the street and bites out of the corners, snow falling upward at the edge. The Lantern is dark (chairs up, OPEN 6 AM), Pike Radio is PIKE & LIND RADIO (TV · VCR · CB · SATELLITE) with televisions and a boom box in the window and a mesh satellite dish on the roof, the sheriff has moved out and HALVARD VIDEO has moved in (posters, NIGHT DROP). A payphone, a newspaper box (NORTH RIDGE ROAD CLOSED), a maroon 1991 sedan and a woodgrain minivan parked in the snow.
- **The back room of Pike & Lind Radio:** a fluorescent tube, pegboard and tools, a beige PC with a C:\> prompt and a dot-matrix printer, a TV in a wood cabinet, rental tapes, QSL cards from everywhere, an OCTOBER 1991 calendar, a percolator on a hot plate, the ham rig showing 2.1400, and eighty strips of electrical tape in rows over it, one for each name, and a blank one with a question mark. If Tomas was sent home in Act 2, there's a crib in a flat box and two cans of yellow paint for tomorrow.

### The dark (Red's chapter)

- Ash Street's basement by touch: Dad's coats on their hooks, his workbench, the gap on the shelf. Then nothing but the dark every station shares, with the sounds of other Halvards coming at you as rings out of the black. Then June's shop in station 44 with the power out, until the pilot lamp comes on.

## Characters at their ages

- **Cal Ostrander at 83:** white receding hair, moustache, glasses, a stoop, a cardigan and a docent's lanyard. Younger in 1980.
- **June Pike at 78:** shorter, more stooped, white hair, still in her cardigan, asleep in the armchair.
- **Tomas** at 21 (shaggy hair, denim jacket), at 24 (side parting, stubble, a paint-flecked sweatshirt), and at about 40 (beard going grey, beanie, patched parka, burned palms, a lamp). Still 13 on the radio.
- **Lise Dahl,** 24 and pregnant, ponytail and scrunchie, a sweater with a zigzag band.
- **Mr. and Mrs. Lund** in their late eighties: his cane and moustache, her bun, glasses, scarf and clipboard for the count.
- **Visitors in 2031** in today's clothes; **Eighty-One** with Sollen's revolver and Red's Tuner slung on a strap (red tape round the handle).
- Older faces now have the lines, jowls, under-eye bags and age spots of their years; young ones don't.

## The story (3.1 to 3.9)

1. **The 2.14 chain.** Warm the frozen batteries on the boiler. In Sollen's log, a sheet in Dad's hand: the patch routing, *A.L. 10/79*. Patch the Tuner into receiver 2 and Dad's relay clicks in: the dial reads years. The needle settles on 2031.
2. **2031.** Through the feed cabin. Cal at 83; Ma's last night at the Lantern on tape ("I still cut two slices"); the key in the hollow desk leg; Dad's photograph, and his car pointing uphill; the plaque, and "her girl used red tape". At 2:14 PM the sound goes and the white comes down. The cassette fades in Mara's hand.
3. **The desk and the chalkboard.** SILENCE: 2031. The key from the leg of Sollen's desk. The needle slides past 2031, and 2009 appears on the board in Mara's handwriting.
4. **2009.** The Rules and the count; Mrs. Lund's question (Mara whistles it the wrong way up); Tomas at forty, outside the line; round the line through the coal chute to the tin behind the boiler. At 2:14 the coupling glows in his hands.
5. **Tomas's notebook.** The fire axe, the wet plaster, the tin, the STATION LOG. Days 17 and 18 again with the control room's own chart recorder: park the dish, cross twice, flat; release it, stand still, climbing. The dish won't stay parked, and grown-up Tomas is calling from 1991.
6. **1991.** Coffee, the street, holding the light while he solders. The roll call at 2:14: "Blue. Zero-seven. Took Red's place at Ash Street. Doesn't know." The close-up of Mara's wrist. **New choice (LETGO):** bless his life, or ask him to come.
7. **Red.** Play as the Mara left in the dark: by touch, then by ear (a wrong sound walks you back round), Dad's whistle, June's storm batteries (fat post to fat post), and the call on 2.14 that a 13-year-old boy answers.
8. **Eighty-One.** TONIGHT on the board, station twenty-three, Red still calling. **Choice 3 (EIGHTY):** pull the cable; or refuse, and either show her the notebook or grab the revolver.
9. **Three knocks** at the front door. END OF ACT 3.

Trips through the feed cabin count as crossings and never raise FRACTURE; climbing in with nothing on the needle frays Mara (+2). Mara's notebook gets the Act 3 notes, two theories that come by themselves and two more to connect.
